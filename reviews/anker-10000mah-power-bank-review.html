Anker 10,000mAh power bank review

A built-in USB-C cable, which is the difference between a power bank you use and one you leave at home.

The verdict: The built-in USB-C cable is the whole argument for this power bank: there is no separate lead to forget, which is the reason most power banks fail their owners. Buy it if your phone dies during long waits and you cannot count on an outlet. Skip it if your centre has accessible outlets at the chairs, where a small wall charger costs a third as much.

What it does well

  • The cable is attached. Every power bank story ends with someone bringing the bank and forgetting the lead, and this design removes that failure entirely.
  • Ten thousand milliamp hours is roughly two phone charges, which covers a long wait, the donation itself and the journey home with room spare.
  • Thirty watt output is fast enough to be worth plugging in during a session rather than only overnight.
  • It fits in a jacket pocket, which matters when the only bag you brought is already holding a blanket and headphones.

Where it falls short

  • The built-in cable is 5.4 inches, so the bank hangs off the phone rather than sitting somewhere separate. In a reclined chair with one arm out of action, that is more awkward than it sounds.
  • One built-in cable means one connector. If your phone or your headphones use anything other than USB-C, you are carrying a second lead anyway and the main advantage disappears.
  • Ten thousand milliamp hours is a modest capacity for a tablet. It will top up a Fire HD 10 but it will not fully charge one from empty.
  • If your centre has outlets at the chairs, this solves a problem you do not have. A wall charger is smaller, cheaper and does not need charging itself.

What owners report

Published owner reviews are consistently positive about the built-in cable, which is the feature people mention first and most often. Charging speed and size both draw praise, and Anker's support record is a recurring reason people choose the brand.

The two most common criticisms are the short cable length, which is inherent to the design, and capacity expectations, where buyers hoped a 10,000 mAh bank would charge a tablet fully. Neither is a fault so much as a specification people did not read.

Who it suits

  • Donors whose phone is the thing they use during a session and who have watched it die during a long wait.
  • Anyone at a centre without accessible outlets at the chairs.
  • People who have owned power banks before and never remembered the cable.

Who should skip it

  • Donors at centres with outlets at every chair, where a wall charger is smaller and cheaper.
  • Anyone charging a tablet from empty, who wants a larger capacity bank.
  • People whose devices are not USB-C, since the built-in cable is then irrelevant.

Common questions

Are there power outlets at plasma donation chairs?

It varies by company and by branch. Some centres have outlets or USB ports at each chair, some have none on the floor. Check yours before spending on a power bank, because a wall charger is cheaper and lighter if outlets are available.

Power bank or wall charger for a donation session?

Wall charger if there is an outlet, power bank if there is not. Our comparison at /compare/power-bank-vs-wall-charger covers cost, weight and the practical question of what actually gets carried.

How many charges will 10,000 mAh give me?

Anker publishes about two full charges for a recent iPhone and slightly under two for a large Android. For a phone used during two donation visits a week, that is a bank you charge every week or two.

Is the built-in cable worth the extra?

For most people, yes. The cheaper model without it costs about $6 less and reintroduces the single most common reason power banks sit unused at home. Six dollars for never forgetting a cable is a good trade.

Can I use a power bank while donating?

Generally yes, since it is a personal item like a phone. Keep everything on the side of your free hand and away from the cannulated arm, and follow any instruction from staff. If you feel unwell at any point, put the phone down and tell them.
