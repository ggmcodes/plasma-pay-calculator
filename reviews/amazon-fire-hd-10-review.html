Amazon Fire HD 10 review

A big, cheap screen for a session that runs long, and the reasons a Kindle may still be the better buy.

The verdict: The Fire HD 10 is the cheapest sensible way to get a large video screen into a donation chair, and downloading before you leave home makes centre wifi irrelevant. Buy it if watching is how you want to spend the time. Skip it if you mainly read, because it is heavier, glossier and worse under overhead lighting than a Kindle that costs about the same.

What it does well

  • A 10.1 inch 1080p screen is genuinely comfortable propped on your lap or against your knees for an hour, in a way a phone is not.
  • Thirteen hours of battery covers several visits between charges, so it is not a device you have to manage.
  • Downloading video at home before you leave removes any dependence on centre wifi, which is frequently slow and shared by everyone on the floor.
  • At about $120 it is the cheapest way to get a large screen, and it is far easier to justify against donation income than a premium tablet.

Where it falls short

  • The screen is glossy, and donation floors are lit from directly overhead. Reflections are a real problem in a reclined chair, and a matte electronic ink reader does not have this issue at all.
  • It is heavy to hold in one hand for an hour. Realistically it lives on your lap or your chest, which limits your viewing angle when reclined.
  • It runs Amazon's Fire OS, not standard Android. Some apps you use daily may simply not be available, and that is worth checking before you buy rather than after.
  • The lower-priced listings carry lock screen advertisements. Removing them is a separate charge, so compare the price of the version you actually want.

What owners report

Published owner reviews are consistent that this is a good tablet for the money and a mediocre tablet in absolute terms. Video playback, battery and build quality are praised. The recurring frustrations are the app store, where people find a familiar app missing, and general responsiveness under load compared with more expensive tablets.

Reports about the advertisements on the lock screen are frequent, and they are a pricing question rather than a hardware fault. Buyers who did not notice the distinction are the ones who complain.

Who it suits

  • Donors who want to watch downloaded shows or films during a session.
  • Anyone who wants a large screen and is unwilling to spend more than about $120 on it.
  • People who share a household device and want something of their own for appointments.

Who should skip it

  • Readers. A Kindle is lighter, glare-free and lasts weeks rather than hours.
  • Anyone who depends on a specific Android app, since Fire OS may not have it.
  • Donors who want to hold the device up one handed for an hour, which this is too heavy for.

Common questions

Is a tablet or an e-reader better for donating plasma?

It depends on whether you watch or read. A tablet is the only one of the two that plays video, and an e-reader is lighter, glare-free and lasts weeks. Our comparison at /compare/e-reader-vs-tablet covers weight, glare, battery and price side by side.

Will I need the centre's wifi?

Not if you download before you leave. Centre wifi is shared across a busy floor and is often slow, so downloading episodes or films at home is the reliable approach and it also avoids using your own mobile data.

Fire HD 10 vs Fire HD 8: which should I buy?

The HD 10 has a bigger, sharper screen. The HD 8 is half the price and lighter, which matters more than it sounds when you are holding it reclined with one arm out of action. If you prop the tablet on your lap, buy the 10. If you hold it, buy the 8.

Does it run normal Android apps?

It runs Fire OS with Amazon's own app store. Many popular apps are there and some are not. Check for the specific apps you rely on before buying, because this is the most common source of buyer regret with these tablets.

How long does the battery last during a donation?

Amazon publishes up to 13 hours of mixed use. A typical donation appointment including screening and the donation itself is well within that, so battery is not the constraint for a single visit.
