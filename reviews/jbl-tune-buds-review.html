What it does well

  • Nothing sits between your head and the chair. In a reclined position that is the single practical advantage over any over-ear pair, and it only becomes obvious about forty minutes into a session.
  • Smart Ambient lets sound back in with a tap, which is how you hear a phlebotomist without taking anything out one handed.
  • Twelve hours in the buds covers any single visit many times over, and the case takes total playback to 48 hours.
  • The case is pocket sized, so there is no bag requirement at all.

Where it falls short

  • In-ear cancellation is shallower than over-ear against the continuous low-frequency noise of separator machines. It reduces the room, it does not remove it.
  • Buds are easy to drop and hard to retrieve one handed while cannulated. If one falls to the floor during a session you are waiting until the end to pick it up.
  • Fit determines everything about how well the cancellation works, and a poor seal makes the feature close to useless. Try the supplied tip sizes properly before deciding they do not work.
  • JBL tuning is bass-forward, which suits music better than speech. If you mostly listen to podcasts or audiobooks, adjust the equaliser or expect a heavy sound.

What owners report

Published owner reviews are positive for the price, with battery life and call quality mentioned most often. Cancellation is generally described as decent rather than exceptional, which is the correct expectation in this price band.

The most common complaints are fit related, particularly for people with smaller ear canals, and occasional reports of one bud disconnecting. Both are common across the whole category rather than specific to this model.

Who it suits

  • Donors who recline fully and find over-ear cups uncomfortable against the headrest.
  • Anyone who wants to carry as little as possible, since the case fits in a pocket.
  • People who listen to audiobooks or podcasts rather than watching, which needs no hands at all.

Who should skip it

  • Donors whose main complaint is machine noise, where over-ear cancellation removes more of it.
  • Anyone who has struggled with in-ear fit before, since the seal decides whether the feature works.
  • People who worry about dropping a bud on a clinic floor while unable to reach it.

Common questions

Are earbuds or headphones better for donating plasma?

Earbuds if you recline, because nothing presses against the chair. Headphones if the machine noise is the problem, because over-ear cancels more of it. Our comparison at /compare/wireless-earbuds-vs-over-ear-headphones covers comfort, cancellation and price together.

Can I wear earbuds during the donation?

Most centres allow it as long as staff can get your attention. Use the ambient mode when someone is working on your arm, and keep the volume where you can hear a person speaking to you. Ask at your centre, since policies vary.

How long does the battery last?

JBL publishes 12 hours in the buds and 48 hours in total with the case, dropping to 10 and 40 with cancelling switched on. A donation appointment is comfortably inside a single charge.

Do they cancel enough noise for a busy centre?

They reduce it rather than remove it. Plasma centres run continuous machinery, which is exactly the noise over-ear pairs handle best. If quiet is the priority over comfort, buy over-ear.

What if a bud falls out during donation?

You will most likely be leaving it on the floor until staff disconnect you, since the cannulated arm has to stay still and reaching across with the other is awkward. Get the tip size right before you rely on them, and consider a corded or over-ear pair if you have dropped buds before.
