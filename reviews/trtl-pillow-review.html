Trtl pillow review

A neck pillow that supports sideways rather than around, and an honest look at the claim on the box.

The verdict: The Trtl holds your head to one side rather than propping it from behind, which is the specific thing that fails in a reclined chair when you doze off. Buy it if your head falling sideways is what makes donating uncomfortable. Skip it if you sit upright and awake, where a $16 memory foam pillow does the same job for a quarter of the money.

What it does well

  • It supports the head sideways, which is the direction a head actually falls when you doze in a reclined chair. A ring pillow supports backwards, which is the direction your head was already supported.
  • At 4.5 oz it is light enough that it genuinely gets carried, and the bag clips to a rucksack.
  • The fleece wrap comes off and goes in a washing machine, which matters for anything worn against the neck and taken repeatedly into a clinical setting.
  • It can be positioned on either side, so it works whichever arm is being used that visit.

Where it falls short

  • The marketing describes it as scientifically proven neck support. Treat that as a manufacturer's claim rather than an independent finding. The design is sensible and many people find it comfortable, and that is a different statement from proof.
  • It is warm. A fleece wrap around your neck in a room where you are already under a blanket can tip from cosy into uncomfortable.
  • It supports one side at a time. If you want to switch sides mid session you have to reposition it, which is fiddly with one hand.
  • Sixty five dollars is a lot for a neck pillow, and a $16 memory foam travel pillow is adequate for most people sitting upright.

What owners report

Published owner reviews split along a clear line. People who bought it because their head falls to the side when they sleep sitting up are consistently positive and often describe it as the only travel pillow that worked for them. People who wanted general neck cushioning are often disappointed and say a normal pillow was more comfortable.

Warmth is the most common complaint across both groups, and sizing comes second, with some owners finding the wrap too short or too long for their neck. There is no size selection to speak of, so that is a real risk.

Who it suits

  • Donors who sleep or doze during a session and wake with a stiff neck.
  • Anyone whose head falls to the side rather than backwards in a reclined chair.
  • People who want something small and washable that lives in a bag permanently.

Who should skip it

  • Donors who stay upright and awake, for whom a cheap memory foam pillow is enough.
  • Anyone who runs warm, since a fleece wrap adds heat where you may not want it.
  • People on a tight budget, where $65 is one to two donations of pay for a pillow.

Common questions

Is a neck pillow useful during a plasma donation?

It depends whether you rest. Sessions commonly run around 45 to 90 minutes in a reclined chair, and people who doze wake with a stiff neck. If you stay upright and occupied you probably do not need one.

Trtl pillow vs a normal travel pillow?

The Trtl holds your head to one side with an internal support. A ring pillow cushions behind and around. If your head falls sideways, the Trtl is the one that addresses it. If you just want padding, the ring pillow is cheaper and cooler.

Neck pillow or a blanket: which makes more difference?

Most donors report cold as the bigger problem than neck comfort, because centres are cool and returned fluid is cooler still. Our comparison at /compare/neck-pillow-vs-travel-blanket sets out which one people actually end up using.

Is the Trtl really scientifically proven?

That is the manufacturer's phrasing and we would not repeat it as fact. The design is reasonable and owner reports from people with the specific problem it addresses are good. Read it as a well-liked product rather than a clinically validated one.

Can I sleep during a plasma donation?

Many centres allow dozing and many donors do. Staff monitor you throughout, but they rely on you reporting how you feel, so if you are prone to feeling faint it is better to stay awake and speak up early. Tell staff immediately if you feel dizzy, sweaty, nauseated or unwell.
