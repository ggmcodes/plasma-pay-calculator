Kindle Paperwhite review

A bigger screen, a warm light and waterproofing, usually for less than the base model.

The verdict: The Paperwhite gives you a larger screen, a warm light and waterproofing, and it frequently costs less than the base Kindle. Buy it if you read at all and want a device you can use one handed while an arm is out of action. Skip it only if you specifically need the smallest and lightest body, or if you want video, which no e-reader does.

What it does well

  • One handed by design. It is light enough to hold in your free hand for an hour and pages with a tap, which is the whole reason an e-reader beats a tablet in a donation chair.
  • A 300 ppi glare-free screen is genuinely readable under the hard overhead lighting on a donation floor, where a glossy tablet reflects the ceiling.
  • The warm light shifts the screen from white towards amber, which many people find easier late in the day.
  • Ten weeks of battery means it lives in a bag and simply works. There is no charger to remember and no session interrupted.

Where it falls short

  • It reads. That is all it does. No video, no meaningful browser and no apps, and if your donation time is when you watch things, this is the wrong purchase.
  • Electronic ink refreshes slowly. Flicking back to check something is noticeably slower than on a screen, though it is irrelevant for linear reading.
  • The 8 GB model is limited if you keep large illustrated books or audiobooks on the device. For plain text it holds far more than anyone reads.
  • The cheaper listings show sponsored screens on the lock screen. Removing them is a separate charge, so compare like for like before you buy.

What owners report

Published owner reviews are strongly positive and unusually uniform: the screen, the battery and the weight are the three things people mention, and the warm light is the feature owners upgrading from older models say they did not expect to care about and then did.

The recurring criticisms concern software rather than hardware, particularly the store interface and advertisements on the cheaper version. Reports of screen damage from pressure in bags appear occasionally, which is a case argument rather than a defect.

Who it suits

  • Donors who read and want a device usable with one hand while the other arm is cannulated.
  • Anyone who currently reads on a phone and finds the glare or the notifications a problem.
  • People who want the best value in the Kindle range, which this usually is.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone who wants video or apps, who should buy a Fire tablet.
  • Readers of comics and illustrated books, who want colour and should look at the Colorsoft.
  • People who genuinely want the smallest possible device, where the base Kindle is lighter.

Common questions

Kindle Paperwhite vs the base Kindle: which should I buy?

The Paperwhite, in almost every case. It has a larger 6.8 inch screen, an adjustable warm light, waterproofing and longer battery, and it is often the cheaper of the two. The base model wins only on size and weight.

Is an e-reader better than a tablet for donating plasma?

For reading, clearly. It is lighter, it pages with one thumb, it does not glare under overhead lights and it lasts weeks. For video a tablet is the only option. Our comparison at /compare/e-reader-vs-tablet works through it properly.

Can I hold a Kindle while donating?

Yes, in your free hand. The cannulated arm stays still and relaxed, and you follow whatever squeezing instruction the staff give. If you feel dizzy, lightheaded, nauseated or unwell at any point, stop and tell staff immediately.

Does the Paperwhite have advertisements?

The lower priced listing does, on the lock screen. There is a version without them for more money, and you can also pay to remove them afterwards. Check which version a listing is before comparing prices.

How long does the battery last in practice?

Amazon publishes up to ten weeks, based on modest daily reading with wireless off and a low light setting. For someone reading during two donation visits a week, that is realistically months between charges.
