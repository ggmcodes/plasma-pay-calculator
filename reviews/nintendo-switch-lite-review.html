Nintendo Switch Lite review

A good handheld console, and an awkward fit for the one thing this site is about.

The verdict: The Switch Lite is a genuinely good, cheap handheld console, and it is the wrong device to bring to a donation chair. Buy it if you want a handheld for the rest of your week and are treating plasma income as the way you are paying for it. Skip it if you are buying it specifically to pass the time while donating, because it needs two hands and one of yours will have a needle in it.

What it does well

  • It is the cheapest way into the Nintendo library, and the library is the reason anyone buys one.
  • Compact and light enough to live in a bag permanently, so it is there when a wait runs long.
  • Local and online wireless multiplayer, which is the part a phone game does not replace.
  • Because it is handheld only, there is no dock, no cables and nothing to set up.

Where it falls short

  • It needs two hands. During a plasma donation one arm is cannulated and must stay still, and many centres ask you to keep that arm relaxed and to squeeze on a cycle. A device with controls on both sides of the screen is close to unusable in that position, and propping it on your chest is not a fix.
  • The screen is not a touch-first interface for most games, so you cannot fall back to one thumb the way you can on a phone.
  • There is no television output on this model. If you expected a console that also plays on a TV, that is the standard Switch, not the Lite.
  • At about $229 it is a discretionary purchase, and on this site it is worth being blunt: if plasma income is covering essentials, a games console is not the thing to buy with it.

What owners report

Owner reviews are broadly positive about what the Lite is: a cheaper, lighter, handheld-only version of a console people already like. The consistent criticisms are the fixed controls, since a drifting stick cannot be swapped the way a detachable controller can, and the absence of television output, which surprises buyers who did not read closely.

For the specific use this page is about, the relevant point is not in any owner review, because owner reviews are not written by people playing with one arm immobilised. It follows from the hardware: two-handed controls and a cannulated arm do not combine.

Who it suits

  • Donors buying a handheld for their own time generally, with plasma income as the funding.
  • People who want the Nintendo library at the lowest entry price and never intend to play on a television.
  • Anyone who wants something in a bag for long waiting-room queues, which is a different problem from the donation itself.

Who should skip it

  • Anyone buying a device specifically for use while connected to the machine. Use an e-reader you can page with one thumb, or headphones and an audiobook.
  • People who want to play on a television. That is the standard Switch, not the Lite.
  • Donors whose plasma income is covering rent, food or fuel. This is a want, and this site should say so.

Common questions

Can you play a Nintendo Switch while donating plasma?

Not comfortably. One arm is cannulated and needs to stay still, and the Switch Lite has controls on both sides of the screen. Most donors who try it give up and switch to something they can hold in one hand. An e-reader or an audiobook is the practical answer.

What can you actually do while donating plasma?

Anything that works one handed: reading on a phone or an e-reader, downloaded video on a tablet propped on your lap, podcasts and audiobooks, or sleeping. The comparison at /compare/e-reader-vs-tablet covers the two most common choices in detail.

Nintendo Switch Lite vs the standard Switch: which should I buy?

The Lite is handheld only and cheaper. The standard Switch docks to a television and has detachable controllers that can be replaced if a stick drifts. If you want to play on a TV at all, the Lite is the wrong model, and no accessory changes that.

How long does the Switch Lite battery last?

Nintendo's published figure is a range that depends heavily on the game, and demanding titles sit at the bottom of it. For a donation visit of an hour or two it is not the limiting factor. The limiting factor is that you cannot hold it.

Is buying a console with plasma money a good idea?

That is your call, not ours, but the honest framing is that a $229 console is roughly four to eight donations depending on your centre and bonus schedule. Our savings goal tracker at /tools/savings-goal-tracker will show you what that looks like against whatever else you were saving for.
