What the bandage is for

The dressing the centre applies after a donation is doing a specific job for a specific period, and the centre tells you how long to keep it on. A bandage from your own box is for afterwards, or for a site that has weeped a little once the original dressing is off. It is not a substitute for following the aftercare instructions. If a site bleeds and does not stop with firm direct pressure, if there is significant swelling, or if you notice spreading redness, warmth or a fever, contact the centre or seek medical attention rather than adding another bandage. Frequently Asked Questions

When can I take the bandage off after donating plasma?

After the period the centre specifies, which they tell you at the end of the appointment and which is commonly a few hours. Taking it off early is a common reason a site starts bleeding again. If you cannot remember the instruction, phone the centre and ask.

Is fabric or plastic better for a donation site?

Flexible fabric conforms to the inside of the elbow and moves with the arm, which is why it stays on better in that position than a rigid plastic strip. That is the main practical difference for this particular use, and it is why most people end up buying the fabric ones.

Should I put ointment under the bandage?

Follow the centre's aftercare instructions rather than adding anything on your own. A clean puncture site that has closed usually needs nothing. If the site is not healing normally, that is a reason to contact the centre or your own doctor rather than to start applying products.

Are there latex-free bandages?

Yes, and most modern adhesive bandages are latex free, but check the box if you have a latex allergy rather than assuming. Tell the centre about any allergy as well, since it affects the materials they use on you during the donation itself.
