When a bruise is not just a bruise

Routine bruising after a donation is common and fades over a week or two. Contact the centre, or seek medical attention, if the swelling is large or growing quickly, if the pain is getting worse rather than better, if your hand becomes numb, weak, cold or pale, if there is spreading redness and warmth or a fever, or if the site bleeds and will not stop with firm pressure. Those signs point to something other than an ordinary bruise and are not for home remedies. This is general information from a site that tracks plasma centre pay rates, and it is not medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does arnica actually work on bruises?

The evidence is mixed. Some trials of topical arnica report a small benefit on bruising and others find no meaningful difference against placebo, and study quality varies. It is popular and generally well tolerated on intact skin, which is a different claim from it being proven effective. If you use it, keep expectations modest.

How long should I ice a bruise?

Standard first aid practice is short applications of around ten to twenty minutes at a time with a barrier between the cold pack and skin, repeated over the first day or so, rather than one long session. Direct prolonged contact with a very cold pack can damage skin. Do not apply cold to an area with reduced sensation.

Why do I bruise so easily at the donation site?

Individual veins, how easily a vein is accessed, and how long pressure was held afterwards all contribute, and some people bruise more readily than others. Telling the staff about it matters, since they can adjust their approach. Easy bruising all over your body, rather than just at the site, is a different question and worth raising with your own doctor.

Can I still donate with a bruise?

That is the centre's decision at your next screening. Many will avoid a bruised area and use the other arm, and some will defer you until it settles. Show them rather than covering it up, since the assessment is theirs to make and hiding a problem serves nobody.
