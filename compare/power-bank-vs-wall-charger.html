One arm, and staying reachable

You will have one arm out of action for most of the appointment, which is worth planning for: put anything you want within reach of your free hand before the process starts, and keep the volume low enough to hear staff. If you begin to feel light-headed, nauseated, sweaty, or notice tingling around your mouth or in your fingers, put the phone down and tell the phlebotomist immediately. Frequently Asked Questions

Do plasma centres have outlets to charge your phone?

Some do and some do not, and where they exist they are often in the wrong place or already in use. It varies by company and by location, so ask on your first visit. Carrying a power bank removes the question entirely.

What size power bank do I need for a donation appointment?

A 10,000 mAh unit covers roughly two full phone charges, which is far more than one appointment needs and keeps the size and weight reasonable. Larger banks are heavier and slower to recharge. If you only want a top-up, a smaller unit is fine.

Can I use my phone during the donation itself?

Most centres allow it. Photography is often restricted for privacy reasons, and staff may ask you to put the phone down while they are setting up or finishing. Keep one hand free and stay attentive to anyone speaking to you.

What if my phone dies and I need to call for a ride?

Ask at reception. Centres deal with this regularly and will usually help you contact someone. Do not drive if you are feeling light-headed after donating; sit down, tell staff, and arrange another way home.
