Protein Bars vs Beef Jerky

Two shelf-stable snacks for the waiting room and afterwards

Verdict

Jerky is the leaner option and carries almost no sugar, which matters because centres ask donors to avoid fatty food before a donation and cloudy plasma is a real cause of deferral. Protein bars carry more calories and are easier to eat quickly, which suits the period straight after a donation. Jerky is high in sodium, which is fine for most people and a genuine issue for anyone whose doctor has told them to limit salt.

Both fit in a bag, keep for months and provide protein without a fridge. They differ on fat, sodium and sugar, and each of those matters for a different part of a donation day.

Fatty food and deferral

Centres commonly ask donors to avoid fatty meals before donating because fat circulating in the blood makes plasma appear cloudy, and cloudy plasma can be rejected. That is a practical instruction from the centre rather than a health warning, and the specific guidance and timing come from the centre you attend. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I eat right before donating plasma?

Most centres want you to have eaten, and specifically want you to avoid fatty food beforehand. Arriving on an empty stomach makes a fainting reaction more likely. The exact window and the specific advice come from your centre, and they will tell you if you ask at reception.

Is beef jerky healthy for regular donors?

It is a lean, shelf-stable protein source, and it is also high in sodium and, in many products, contains preservatives. For most people eating it occasionally that is unremarkable. If you have high blood pressure or have been told to limit salt, ask your doctor how it fits your diet rather than treating it as a free protein source.

Which has more protein per dollar?

Protein powder beats both comfortably. Between these two, bars generally provide more protein per package, though jerky provides it with far less fat and sugar. If protein per dollar is the actual goal, buy powder and keep these for portability.

Do plasma centres provide snacks?

Many provide water, juice and a snack after donating, and some run promotions around it. Policies vary by company and by location. Eating something afterwards is standard advice, and if you feel light-headed the staff will want you to stay seated and take something before you leave.
