Choose the Compression Arm Sleeve if…

A compression sleeve is a general garment some people wear for arm support or warmth, and it is machine washable and reusable. It is not designed for a puncture site and should not be pulled over a fresh bandage, where it can shift the dressing or apply uneven pressure. If you want to wear one on a donation day, put it on after the centre has cleared you to remove the bandage rather than over the top of it.

Choose the Self-Adherent Cohesive Bandage Wrap, 15 Rolls if…

Cohesive wrap is the stretchy self-adhering material centres commonly use, and it is worth having a roll at home for the times a bandage works loose. It sticks to itself rather than to skin or hair, which makes it comfortable and easy to remove. The failure mode is applying it too tight: if the hand tingles, goes cold, changes colour or swells, take it off immediately, because that is a circulation problem rather than a bandage that needs a moment to settle.

Aftercare comes from the centre

Each centre gives specific aftercare instructions, including how long to keep the dressing on, when you can lift with that arm, and what to do if the site starts bleeding. Follow those rather than a general article. Get in touch with the centre, or seek medical attention, if the site bleeds and does not stop with firm pressure, if there is significant or rapidly growing swelling, if pain gets worse over the following days, if the hand becomes numb or weak, or if you develop spreading redness, warmth or a fever. Those are the situations where a bandage is not the answer. This site tracks plasma pay rates and does not give medical advice.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long should I leave the bandage on after donating plasma?

For the period the centre specifies, which is commonly a few hours and which they will tell you at the end of the appointment. Removing it early is a common cause of the site reopening. If you cannot remember what they said, phone and ask rather than guessing.

Can I lift weights after donating plasma?

Centres generally advise against strenuous activity and heavy lifting for a period after donating, particularly with the donation arm, and they will give you a timeframe. The concern is the site reopening and bruising. Follow their instruction, which is based on what they have just done to your arm.

Is it normal for the site to bruise every time?

Bruising is a common side effect and some people bruise more readily than others. If it happens at every donation, or bruises are large, tell the staff, since they may change the vein used or how the site is handled. Persistent easy bruising elsewhere on your body is a different matter and worth raising with your own doctor.

What if my hand tingles after donating?

Tingling around the mouth and in the fingers during a donation is a recognised reaction to the citrate anticoagulant and staff manage it routinely, but they need to be told while it is happening. Tingling or numbness in the hand after a donation, particularly with weakness, is a reason to contact the centre rather than wait, and it can indicate a problem at the needle site.
