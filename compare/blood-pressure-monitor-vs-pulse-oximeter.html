Blood Pressure Monitor vs Pulse Oximeter

Two home devices, and what neither of them is for

Verdict

A blood pressure monitor is the more useful of the two for most people, because blood pressure is one of the vital signs checked at every plasma donation and a reading outside the centre's range means a deferral. A pulse oximeter measures blood oxygen saturation, which is not what defers plasma donors, and readings are less reliable in cold hands, with nail polish and on darker skin. Neither device diagnoses anything. Take unusual readings to your own doctor.

Both are cheap consumer medical devices and both get bought by donors trying to understand a deferral. They measure completely different things.

Choose the Upper Arm Blood Pressure Monitor if…

Choose an upper arm monitor if blood pressure is the reason you are being turned away, since knowing your usual numbers over days is far more informative than a single reading taken after you rushed to an appointment. Use an upper arm cuff rather than a wrist device, sit quietly for several minutes first, keep your arm supported at heart level, and take readings at the same times each day. Bring the log to your own doctor rather than interpreting it yourself.

A home reading is not a diagnosis

Consumer devices are useful for spotting a pattern and useless for diagnosing anything. If your home blood pressure readings are consistently high or low, or if you get a reading that alarms you, that goes to your own doctor rather than into a decision about whether to turn up at a centre. On donation day the centre carries out the screening the FDA requires and their staff decide whether you can donate, and no home device overrides that. Seek urgent medical attention for chest pain, severe shortness of breath, fainting, or symptoms of a stroke rather than reaching for a monitor. Frequently Asked Questions

What blood pressure do you need to donate plasma?

Centres apply defined acceptable ranges for blood pressure, pulse and temperature as part of the screening, and readings outside their range mean a deferral for that day. The exact thresholds are the centre's to apply, so ask them what your reading was and what range they use. A single high reading after rushing in is common and is not the same as having high blood pressure.

Why was I deferred for blood pressure?

Because the reading taken at that visit fell outside the range the centre accepts. It can reflect stress, caffeine, rushing, or an underlying condition. Ask the staff for the number, sit quietly and ask whether they will retake it, and if readings are consistently outside the range, see your own doctor rather than trying different centres.

Are pulse oximeters accurate?

They are useful trend devices with real limitations. Accuracy is affected by cold hands, poor circulation, movement, nail polish and skin pigmentation, and regulators have issued specific warnings that readings can be less accurate in people with darker skin. Never make a decision about seeking care based on the number alone if you feel unwell.

Should I check my own vitals before going to a centre?

It can save a wasted trip if you have been deferred before for a vital sign, and knowing your usual range is genuinely useful information to bring to your doctor. It does not replace anything the centre does, and it is not worth turning into a source of anxiety. The centre will take its own readings regardless.
