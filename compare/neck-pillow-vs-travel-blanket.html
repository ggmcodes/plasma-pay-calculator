Feeling cold, and feeling unwell

Being cold in a cold room is ordinary. Feeling suddenly cold, clammy or sweaty during or after a donation is different, and along with light-headedness, nausea and a feeling of weakness it can be the start of a fainting reaction, which is one of the more common events centres deal with. Tell the staff straight away and stay seated or lying down until they say otherwise. Do not try to walk it off, and do not drive if you feel unwell after leaving. Frequently Asked Questions

Why are plasma centres so cold?

Cool temperatures are common in clinical environments and equipment rooms, and centres are often large open spaces with a lot of machinery. Whatever the reason, the practical consequence is that donors get cold sitting still for an hour, which is why so many regulars bring layers or a blanket.

Can I bring my own blanket to a plasma centre?

Usually yes, and many donors do. Policies vary by company so it is worth asking on a first visit. If your centre provides blankets and you would rather use your own, nobody is likely to object.

What should I wear to donate plasma?

A short sleeve or loose sleeve top that lets staff access the inside of the elbow easily, plus a layer you can put on afterwards because the floor is cold. Comfortable clothing you can sit in for an hour or more. Avoid tight sleeves that have to be pushed up and then constrict the arm.

Is it normal to feel cold after donating?

Feeling slightly chilled afterwards is common, and centres will usually offer something to drink and a place to sit. Feeling cold and clammy along with light-headedness or nausea is a different signal and should be reported to staff before you leave the building.
